§ 0 Reading the numbers

Results in this ledger come from two archives produced months apart under different training recipes. They share one test set exactly, which is what makes them comparable at all — and the differences are what stop them being comparable in every respect. Read this section before quoting anything below.

0.1

The common test set

Every number in the leaderboard is measured on the same 3,970 frames: every clip whose day-of-month is 22 or 24, held out before any split is drawn. It is a natural split — no balancing or reduction is ever applied to it — and it decomposes as 3,156 day-22 frames plus 814 day-24 UFC frames, over 118 clips.

Class composition. Verified identical between the two archives before they were merged — this check is what licenses the combined table.

0.2

What each metric means

joint accuracy

Fraction of frames where both heads are simultaneously correct. The strictest measure, and the one to rank by — it is what the downstream route decision actually depends on.

obstacle / deviation accuracy

Per-head accuracy, treated independently. Always higher than joint. A high obstacle score with a low joint score means the deviation head is carrying the failure.

macro_f1_presentes

Macro F1 over only the classes with non-zero support. Necessary on the janela sets, which contain no free_path and no none frames — plain macro F1 there averages in classes that cannot occur.

route accuracy

The two heads decoded into one four-way decision: forward = free_path+none, stop = obstacle+none, left/right = the deviation regardless of obstacle. The end-user-facing metric.

Series A Provenance audits

Before any model was trained, three questions about the existing numbers had to be settled: where the paper's split table comes from, why the trainers report more rows than they have frames, and whether the labels on Drive are correct. All three were read out of the code and reproduced against archived run artifacts.

A1

Where the paper's split table actually comes from

Discrepancy found
What
Traced the Train / Validation / Test triple printed in the WiP paper back through the training scripts to the three Kaggle inputs, then rebuilt it locally.
Why
A split table is the first thing a reviewer checks. It had to be reproducible from an archived artifact, not from memory.
How
Read load_allframe_rows(), grouped_split() and make_head_balanced_rows(); replayed the pipeline and compared every field against anamariabarrosnonato/ss2ere → sibgrapi/run4/final_statistics1.txt.
Conclusion
The pool is 52,719 frames over 608 clips (dataset8 22,781 + dataset9 814 + auto-trim 29,124). Test is 3,970, natural and unreduced. The paper's table mixes two different quantities — Train and Validation are post-reduction row counts, Test is the raw split. And the printed triple (33,188 / 6,102 / 3,970) matches no archived run: run4 reads 33,118 / 6,172 / 3,970, same policy and pool, a different clip draw.
A2

Why 24,347 frames are reported as 33,118 rows

Explained
What
Established what one "training sample" is when the two heads are balanced independently.
Why
Without this the split counts cannot be interpreted, and no materialized one-image-per-frame dataset can reproduce them.
How
Read the reduction and masked-loss code, then counted per-head membership for every frame in run4's split.
Conclusion
A single class balance cannot serve both heads, because free_path is always none. So the balance runs twice, and a frame kept by both is emitted as two rows: 8,771 × 2 + 10,293 + 5,283 = 33,118, over 24,347 distinct frames. Labels are identical on both copies; only which head's loss the row feeds differs. Duplicated frames get twice the backbone exposure per epoch, four times after the flip.
A3

A non-unique join key silently relabels 4,537 frames

Bug confirmed
What
Audited how the two trainer families pair the obstacle_freepath and deviation_lrn manifests.
Why
Cross-checking the Drive sense dataset against the run statistics turned up deviation counts that did not match.
How
Replayed both join keys over deviation_lrn/labels.csv (24,847 rows) and compared the resulting class totals.
Conclusion
_pair_task_dirs() joins on (source_clip, filename), which is unique. read_lateral_labels() in the ConvLSTM datagen joins on (source_clip, frame_index), which is not — the builder strips the _head/_tail suffix on purpose, so a cut frame and an endpoint frame of the same recording collide. Last-wins relabels 2,267 left and 2,270 right frames as none. Endpoint frames carry no day-22 or day-24 clips, so the test split is clean — but sense/treino/labels.txt on Drive is affected, and so is everything downstream of it (see B3).

Series B The run archive

Results predating this campaign, recovered from Desktop\sibgrapi\ and its copy under sense_run4_experiments\referencia\. This is where the mobilenetv1, resnet50 and efficientnet_b0 numbers live — and where three things needed correcting before they could be used.

B1

Sixteen files, nine runs

De-duplicated
What
Catalogued sibgrapi_run_stats/run{1..4}_{0..3}.txt — nominally four runs × four architectures — and checked whether they are distinct.
Why
The naming implies four independent seeds per architecture, which would support a mean and a variance. If true, that is exactly what the paper needs.
How
Hashed all sixteen files and grouped by content.
Conclusion
Only nine are unique. run2_0 = run3_0 = run4_0, run2_2 = run3_2 = run4_2, run2_3 = run3_3 = run4_3 and run2_1 = run3_1 — re-archived copies, not repeated runs. That leaves three mobilenetv1 results and two each for the others. No variance claim can be made from this archive, and any mean over "four runs" is a mean over duplicates.
This corrects a number that was briefly reported. A first pass over these files computed a per-architecture mean ± standard deviation across all sixteen. Those figures treated duplicates as independent samples and are void. The leaderboard above uses the nine de-duplicated runs only.
B2

Four CNN architectures on the common test set

Backbone is not the driver
What
Extracted the test block from each of the nine unique runs: efficientnet_b0, mobilenetv1, mobilenetv4 and resnet50, all two-head, all under the per-head masking regime.
Why
To answer the architecture question directly, and to place the campaign's mobilenetv4 and dinov2 results in a wider field than they had.
How
Confirmed each run's test block sums to 3,970 with the exact class composition of §0.1, then merged into the leaderboard.
Conclusion
Best per family: mobilenetv1 0.9453, mobilenetv4 0.9355, resnet50 0.8690, efficientnet_b0 0.8275. But every family's internal range exceeds the gap between families — resnet50 alone spans 0.5678 to 0.8690. The backbone is not what determines the outcome here; the regime and the split are.
A revealing pattern in the weakest runs. run1_3 (resnet50) scores 0.8945 on the obstacle head but 0.6060 on deviation, for a joint of 0.5678. run1_2 (mobilenetv4) is the same shape: obstacle 0.9668, deviation 0.7884. Across the whole archive the obstacle head is near-saturated and stable, and essentially all the variance lives in the deviation head — which is the harder, rarer, and more label-noise-prone task. Joint accuracy is therefore very nearly a measurement of the deviation head alone.
B3

Random-frame splits inflate accuracy by six points

Leakage measured
What
A separate family of runs in sibgrapi\mobilenetv4\: mobilenetv4 trained under two split strategies — random_frames (frames shuffled into train/val/test) and random_clips (whole clips assigned) — five seeds each, scored by route accuracy at 30 and 10 FPS.
Why
This is the frame-level-versus-clip-level split question the paper raises, run as a controlled experiment with real repeated seeds — the only place in the whole archive where variance is genuinely measurable.
How
final_route_accuracy30fps.json and ...10fps.json, decoding both heads into forward / left / right / stop.
Conclusion
At 30 FPS, shuffling frames scores 0.9647 ± 0.0040 against 0.9049 ± 0.0271 for splitting by clip — six points of pure leakage, and a standard deviation almost seven times smaller. Frame-level splitting does not merely inflate the score; it also manufactures apparent stability, because adjacent near-duplicate frames land on both sides of the split.
Route accuracy, five seeds per cell. The 10 FPS row is the mechanism check: subsample away the near-duplicates and most of the gap closes.

The 10 FPS row is the part that makes this conclusive rather than merely suggestive. If the six-point gap were a real difficulty difference between the two split types, thinning the frames would not touch it. Instead the gap collapses to 1.3 points — exactly what the near-duplicate-leakage explanation predicts, since subsampling is precisely the removal of the frames that leak.

These runs sit on the A3 bug. Their dataset_used_dir is convlstm_mobilenetv1_all_frames/dataset_used — output of the datagen with the non-unique join, so roughly 4,537 lateral frames in the training pool are mislabelled none. And their test sets are their own random draws (6,781 and 3,062 frames), not the common 3,970. That is why none of these rows appear in the leaderboard. The frames-versus-clips comparison survives both problems, because each side is affected identically.
B4

ConvLSTM: a data generator, not a scored model

No result exists
What
Searched every archive for a ConvLSTM result on the common test set.
Why
ConvLSTM appears throughout the scripts and the wiki, so it reads like a tested architecture. It is worth knowing whether it ever produced a comparable number.
How
Grepped every JSON and statistics file on the Desktop and under data/ for ConvLSTM results and for test blocks sized 3,970.
Conclusion
None exists. convlstm_mobilenetv1_all_frames.py is a data generator — the one carrying the A3 join bug — whose dataset_used/ output feeds other trainers; that is the only sense in which "ConvLSTM" appears in the results. A separate train_convlstm_classifier.py exists in scripts/ but has produced no archived run. ConvLSTM is untested on this task, and any claim about it in the paper would be unsupported.

Series D Dataset construction

Five datasets, all materialized at 224×224 in the Drive sense file tree and label style, all carrying a proveniencia.csv mapping each sequential filename back to its clip, frame and source video — the sequential renaming would otherwise destroy provenance.

D1

Bit-exact reproduction of run4

33,118 / 6,172 / 3,970
What
Rebuilt run4's MobileNetV1 split as real image folders outside the repo.
Why
Every later experiment needed a fixed, inspectable reference partition rather than a seed that has to be guessed.
How
Replayed the A1 pipeline, taking the validation clip set from the archived run's own background_reduction clip lists instead of re-drawing it.
Conclusion
Every field matches. Because the partition is the run's own, later variants are comparable to the published numbers rather than to a fresh draw.
D2

Distinct frames, one image per frame

24,347 train
What
The same split with each source frame appearing exactly once, carrying both labels.
Why
The A2 duplication is an artifact of loss masking, not of the data. Dropping it makes "one row = one image" true again.
How
Collapsed the duplicated rows; proveniencia.csv gains a cabecas column recording which heads had kept each frame.
Conclusion
This became the baseline configuration for every training run in Series E.
D3

Distinct frames plus mirrored copies

48,694 train
What
D2 with a horizontally flipped copy of every training frame, left ⇄ right swapped.
Why
To materialize on disk the augmentation the trainers apply in memory, so it can be inspected and reused.
How
aplicar_flip.py ships inside the folder and regenerates the effective training set on demand.
Conclusion
Used as a materialization aid; the Series E runs apply the flip deterministically at train time instead.
D4

Deviation windows extended 0.5 s past the cut

10,047 / 1,930 / 2,706
What
For every clip labelled left or right, the window [start, end + 0.5 s] decoded from the original recording — a 1.0 s cut becomes 1.5 s — with every frame kept.
Why
The cut ends where the annotator stopped it, but the manoeuvre is still visually legible just after. The question was whether those frames carry usable signal.
How
Resolved 327 of 328 clips: 301 from cut_info.json, 24 from history.json plus the July-24 provenance, and 3 recovered by matching the export's first frame against its source recording — validated to −0.010…−0.024 s, under one frame at 30 fps.
Conclusion
Built and used as the uniao configuration, with a known caveat: 899 of its 2,706 test frames are flagged pos_corte=1, where the manoeuvre is over but the label still says left/right. D5 and V2 exist to measure that caveat.
D5

Deviation windows extended exactly 10 frames

8,957 / 1,720 / 2,407
What
D4 rebuilt with the tail counted in decoded frames rather than seconds: [start, end] plus the next 10 frames.
Why
Two reasons. A frame-counted tail is exact and independent of frame rate, where 0.5 s was ~15 frames at 30 fps. And a shorter tail should contain fewer of the mislabelled frames D4 flagged.
How
The decode loop gates on a per-clip post-cut counter instead of a timestamp. The frame-matching recovery of the three July-24 clips was ported as well, so both datasets cover the same 60 test clips.
Conclusion
13,084 frames over 327 clips, with exactly 10 post-cut frames per clip and zero short tails (224 × 10, 43 × 10, 60 × 10). Scored in V2.

Series E Campaign training runs

Eleven models on Kaggle GPU. Every run uses run4's configuration — 224×224, batch 32, AdamW lr 1e-4 / wd 1e-4, ReduceLROnPlateau, fp16 AMP, 40 epochs, early-stop patience 6, best state by validation loss, seed 43, deterministic horizontal flip on train only — with exactly one deliberate change per experiment. Per-head masking is dropped throughout: every frame carries both labels and trains both heads.

E1

Full rate — the reference pair

Best campaign CNN
What
MobileNetV4 conv_small, two configurations: baseline (D2, 24,347 train) and uniao (D2 + D4, 34,394 train).
Why
To establish the reference against which every subsampling variant is measured, and to test whether the extended windows help.
How
Kernel sensecv-mobilenetv4-two-head, nothing else changed.
Conclusion
full_rate/uniao leads every campaign CNN view — joint 0.9156 against 0.8806 for baseline, and the best obstacle accuracy of any model anywhere (0.9746). But see E4: that advantage is measured under a validation split that is not measuring generalization.
E2

5 FPS subsampling

Costs accuracy
What
Every split subsampled from 30 to 5 FPS, both configurations.
Why
Consecutive frames at 30 FPS are near-duplicates. If a 6× smaller dataset trains as well, every future collection and training run gets cheaper.
How
Kernel sensecv-mobilenetv4-5-fps, per-clip even subsampling on train, validation and test alike.
Conclusion
It costs accuracy: baseline falls to 0.8103. And fps5/uniao collapsed outright to 0.5861, best epoch 2 of 8 — early stopping fired before the deviation head converged on a training set four times smaller. E5 later showed this was an optimization failure, not a limit of the data.
E3

DINOv2 cosine selection at 0.98

Worse than 5 FPS
What
Frames selected per clip by DINOv2 ViT-S/14 cosine distance, keeping a frame only when it differs from the last kept one by more than the 0.98 threshold.
Why
A content-aware alternative to fixed-rate subsampling — in principle it should drop redundancy more intelligently than a clock does.
How
Kernel sensecv-mobilenetv4-dinov2-0-98, selection applied to every split.
Conclusion
Worse than 5 FPS despite keeping nearly twice as many frames (11,057 vs 6,237): baseline 0.7307. Selection removes the most redundant frames, which are the clear none ones — starving the majority class the model calibrates against. A second finding fell out of it: the selection is not a per-frame property. The same threshold keeps 953 frames over the distinct set but 1,382 of those same frames when the janela frames are in the pool, because the greedy rule compares against the last kept frame. Any "DINOv2-selected dataset" must state the pool it was selected from.
E4

Held-out-day validation

Leakage confirmed
What
Validation replaced by an entire collection day, 21-06-2026, instead of a clip-grouped draw from the same pool.
Why
Clip-grouped validation was returning a validation loss of 0.0003, which is not a plausible number for this task. The suspicion was that validation clips share corridors and sessions with training clips.
How
Kernel sensecv-mobilenetv4-held-out-day-validation. 21-06-2026 was chosen because it is the only day with real support for all four label combinations (3,913 frames, 9.6% of the pool); every other candidate is deviation-only or clear-only.
Conclusion
The single most important result of the campaign. Validation loss is 0.275 for baseline and 1.186 for uniao, against 0.0003 clip-grouped — roughly a thousand times higher on the same task. And the consequence is direct: the union's advantage does not survive the honest split — dia_validacao/uniao (0.7856) falls below dia_validacao/baseline (0.8710), reversing E1's ordering.
Two independent measurements of the same phenomenon. E4 varies the validation split and finds a 1000× loss difference; B3 varies the test split across five seeds and finds six points of route accuracy. Different data, different metric, different year — same conclusion. Together they are a far stronger argument for the paper than either alone.
E5

DINOv2 fine-tuning ladder

Best on the janela sets
What
DINOv2 ViT-S/14 two-head on exactly E2's fps5/uniao data path, at three unfreezing depths: ultimos1 (1.78 M trainable, 8.1%), ultimos2 (3.55 M, 16.1%) and completo (22.06 M, 100%).
Why
To separate backbone capacity from data volume. Since only the backbone and what is unfrozen change, the comparison against E2's MobileNetV4 is clean.
How
Kernels sensecv-dinov2-5-fps-uniao and sensecv-dinov2-5-fps-uniao-last-1, run4's configuration throughout.
Conclusion
Unfreezing one block is enough, and a second does not help. ultimos1 beats ultimos2 with half the trainable parameters, and it rescues E2's collapse — 0.8965 against MobileNetV4's 0.5861 on identical data, training on 8,029 frames instead of 34,394. completo scored 0.4426 but did not diverge: train loss fell monotonically and validation deviation F1 was still rising when patience ran out. lr 1e-4 is roughly 10× too high for a full ViT-S fine-tune. The available conclusion is "full fine-tuning does not converge at run4's learning rate", not "full fine-tuning is worse".
Error structure differs by backbone. The MobileNetV4 models essentially never invert direction — their errors are a fallback to none. DINOv2 ultimos2 makes genuine inversions (77 left→right). ultimos1 is better behaved: 11 and 4. For assistive navigation the distinction is not cosmetic — a none fallback is silent, an inversion steers the person the wrong way.

Series V Evaluation

Two scoring passes over the campaign checkpoints. In both, each model predicts once over the union of all unique frames and every test view is a subset of those predictions, so the views are mutually consistent by construction.

V1

Six test sets against eight models

Ranking is stable
What
Every MobileNetV4 checkpoint scored on six views: the distinct test set, its 5 FPS subsample and its DINOv2-selected subset, each with and without the janela frames added.
Why
To find out how much a reported accuracy depends on the choice of test set — and therefore how much a single quoted number is worth.
How
eval_sense_matrix.py, one prediction pass per model over 6,676 unique frames.
Conclusion
Absolute scores move by up to 15 points with the test set; the ranking barely changes. A number quoted without naming its test set is meaningless, but a comparison between models is robust. Two corollaries: the 5 FPS view ranks models identically to the full set (within 0.005 for every model), so it is a safe cheap proxy — while the DINOv2 view shifts scores 3–6 points and reorders pairs, so it is not.
V2

Does a shorter tail carry cleaner labels?

Yes — 10 beats 15
What
All eleven checkpoints scored on nine views spanning both janela datasets, including the pre-cut and post-cut halves of each in isolation.
Why
D4's post-cut frames are labelled by assumption, not observation. Splitting the metric separates model error from the labelling assumption — and settles whether 10 frames is a better cut-off than 0.5 s.
How
eval_sense_janela10.py, one prediction pass per model over 9,083 unique frames, importing V1's metric definitions so the numbers stay comparable.
Conclusion
The design check passed exactly: the 1,807 pre-cut frames score identically in both datasets for all eleven models, to four decimals — proof the two differ only in the tail. And on the tail itself, frames 11–15 are measurably worse labelled than frames 1–10 for 8 of 11 models, mean drop 1.4 points. The three exceptions are the three weakest models, which fail across the whole tail. 10 frames is the better cut-off.

Outcome What carries to the paper

1

Split leakage, measured two independent ways

Vary the validation split and loss goes from 0.0003 clip-grouped to 0.275 on a genuinely held-out day — roughly a thousandfold. Vary the test split across five seeds and route accuracy goes from 0.9647 on shuffled frames to 0.9049 on whole clips — six points. Two different experiments, run months apart on different data with different metrics, agreeing. The 10 FPS control closes the argument: thin out the near-duplicate frames and the gap collapses to 1.3 points, which is what the leakage explanation predicts and what no rival explanation does.

2

The union's advantage does not survive an honest split

Under clip-grouped validation the extended-window union wins (0.9156 against 0.8806). Under held-out-day validation it loses (0.7856 against 0.8710). Any claim about the union has to name which validation regime produced it.

3

Architecture is not what drives the result

Five architectures, twenty results, one test set. The best four families land within 0.118 of each other, while a single family spans up to 0.454 internally depending on regime, data variant and where early stopping fires. And nearly all of that variance lives in the deviation head — the obstacle head is near-saturated across the board. Effort spent on the split and the labels buys more than effort spent on the backbone.

4

The split table mixes two different quantities

Train and Validation are post-reduction row counts where frames kept by both heads are counted twice; Test is the raw natural split. Presented in one table headed "extracted frames" this is misleading — the natural pool is 41,727 / 7,022 / 3,970. Separately, the printed triple matches no archived run.

5

Post-cut labels decay with distance from the cut

Measured, not assumed: frames 11–15 past the cut score 1.4 points worse than frames 1–10, consistently across the models that work at all. The left/right label stops being true somewhere in that range. A 10-frame tail is the better operating point, and the pre-cut identity check makes the comparison airtight.

6

Errors are overwhelmingly benign

On the 10-frame tail the leading model makes 4 direction inversions in 600 frames (0.67%); the rest of its error is a fallback to none. For an assistive navigation aid that distinction is the whole safety argument — a silence is recoverable, a wrong turn is not — and it belongs in the paper next to the accuracy figures.
